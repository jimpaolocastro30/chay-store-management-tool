1. What Chay Ops does

Chay Ops is a web app for Philippine small businesses. It keeps day-to-day store numbers in one place so you can see cash, stock, and profit without a spreadsheet maze.

  • Record daily sales (cash, GCash, Maya, card, bank transfer)
  • Log operating expenses and inventory losses
  • Track SKUs, quantities, costs, and low-stock alerts
  • Record capital in, extra investment, and owner withdrawals
  • Preview and export P&L, inventory value, KPI, and capital reports
  • Control access with Owner, Manager, and Staff roles

Open the app in a browser at http://localhost:3000 (local) or your deployed URL. The layout is mobile-friendly and can be installed as a PWA from the browser.

2. Getting started

Sign in

  1. Go to the login page (/login).
  2. Enter your email and password.
  3. Click Enter dashboard.

On a fresh database with no users yet, click Load demo data first. That creates sample accounts, inventory, sales, expenses, and capital so you can explore immediately.

5. Dashboard

The Dashboard is the daily health check. Cards include:

  • Revenue MTD / YTD — sales this month and this year
  • Net Profit MTD — revenue minus expenses and losses, with net margin
  • Gross Margin and expense ratio
  • Cash Position and working capital
  • Inventory Value and active SKU count
  • Low Stock Alerts — items at or below reorder level
  • Inventory Turnover — COGS ÷ inventory value (month-to-date)
  • ROI on Capital — return versus capital deployed

Below the cards you will see a 6-month revenue vs expenses chart, this month’s expense mix, a low-stock list, and recent alerts. Use Manage to jump to Inventory or View all for Alerts.

If the Dashboard shows an error and MongoDB is empty, open Login and click Load demo data.

6. Revenue

Use Revenue to record collections as they happen (or at end of day).

  1. Open Revenue.
  2. Enter Amount (PHP).
  3. Write a short Description (for example, “Walk-in tea sales”).
  4. Confirm the Date.
  5. Choose a Payment method: Cash, GCash, Maya, Card, or Bank transfer.
  6. Add an optional Reference (OR number, GCash ref, etc.).
  7. Click Save revenue.

Saved entries appear in Recent revenue with a running total of listed sales. Enter one row per batch or till close — whichever matches how your store counts the day.

7. Expenses and losses

Open Expenses to log operating costs and inventory damage.

  1. Set Type to Expense or Loss / damage.
  2. Enter amount, category, description, and date.
  3. Click Save entry.

Categories: COGS, rent, utilities, payroll, marketing, supplies, transport, damage, other.

Use Expense for rent, salaries, restocking, and utilities. Use Loss / damage when stock is broken, expired, or otherwise written off. Both reduce profit; losses are meant for inventory write-offs rather than ordinary bills.

8. Inventory

Add an item

  1. Open Inventory.
  2. Fill SKU, name, category, quantity, reorder level, unit cost, sell price, and location (default: Main Store).
  3. Click Add inventory item.

SKU should be unique (for example TEA-001). Selling price may be 0 for supplies you do not sell.

Search and adjust stock

Search by SKU, name, or category, then click Search. On each row, + and − change quantity by one. Quantity at or below reorder level is highlighted in amber and counted on the Dashboard as a low-stock alert.

Import CSV / Excel

Click Import CSV/XLSX and choose a file. Rows are matched on SKU (upsert). Suggested columns:

9. Capital

Owners use Capital to record money put into the business and money taken out.

  • Initial capital — starting funds
  • Additional investment — later cash-in
  • Withdrawal — owner draw

The page shows Invested, Withdrawn, and Net capital. Save an amount, description, and date to append the ledger. Net capital feeds Dashboard ROI and cash-related KPIs.

10. Reports

Owners and managers can generate:

  • Profit & Loss
  • Inventory valuation
  • KPI summary
  • Capital movements
  1. Choose report type.
  2. Optionally set From and To dates.
  3. Click Preview to view on screen, or Excel/CSV to download a file that opens in Excel.

Use date ranges for monthly close or investor packs. Leave dates blank to use the default period for that report.

11. Alerts

Alerts cover low stock, cash warnings, KPI notices, and info messages. Unread items are highlighted. You can mark one alert as read or click Mark all read.

After demo data is loaded you should see a welcome message plus any low-stock items that already sit under their reorder level (for example Earl Grey, bottled cold brew, and paper cups in the sample catalog).

12. Users

Only the Owner can open Users. To invite someone:

  1. Enter full name, email, a temporary password, and role (owner / manager / staff).
  2. Click the save / invite action.

Give the person their email and temporary password, then ask them to sign in at the login page. Share only the access they need: Staff for encoding, Manager for operations and reports, Owner for capital and user admin.

14. Daily workflow and tips

  1. Morning: Sign in as Staff or Manager. Check Dashboard low stock and Alerts.
  2. During the day: Encode revenue as tills close. Log purchases and bills on Expenses as receipts arrive.
  3. Stock: Use + / − for small adjustments; import a file after a full count.
  4. Week / month end: Manager or Owner opens Reports → P&L and KPI, then downloads CSV for the accountant or investor.
  5. Owner only: Record extra capital or withdrawals on the same day they happen so cash position stays honest.
Encode in PHP. Use consistent descriptions (“Store rent”, “Staff salaries”) so expense charts stay readable. Keep SKUs stable so imports update the same items instead of creating duplicates.
